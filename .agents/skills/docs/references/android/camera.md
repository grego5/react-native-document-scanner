# Android camera

## Document camera

- The view owns CameraX binding, capture admission and presentation. It borrows the host session. Camera use requires an active, attached foreground view. Preview, analysis and still capture share a viewport.
- Capture output and processing use the settings admitted at the press. Live focus and guide settings govern readiness. Rebinding waits for capture settlement.
- [Shared engine](../engine.md) owns guide acceptance and gate contracts. [Android validation](validation.md) owns hardware checks and trace workflows.

### Focus ownership

- `FocusController` owns lens commands, tap/capture ownership and each scan's whole attempt: the frames that prove its lock, tied to the scan's token, one resend of a lost trigger, its deadline and its release. A release asked for during a still waits for the shot to settle, since cancelling would move the lens mid-shot. The page print target is stored in page coordinates so flow can carry it. Metering fits inside the page. If a box cannot fit, focus uses the frame centre.
- Optical flow only carries the guide's outline and paces its inference. Motion for focus and capture comes from the motion sensors, since flow over soft frames, before focus is found or in the dark, reads motion a still hand does not make: the focus cycle's steady hold and the print spot's pick wait for the gyroscope and accelerometer to read quiet over 300 ms, and the capture's framing checks both sensors per frame interval while its smear check reads the gyroscope. The accelerometer is needed because the gyroscope sees rotation, not a move along or toward the page. A running sensor's missing reading is unknown motion, never quiet. Without the accelerometer the focus cycle falls back to the capture's steadiness and framing judges rotation alone; without a gyroscope the capture's motion checks are off.
- Both cameras run one focus cycle, `FocusCycle`, with one set of rules in `FocusRules`: the steady hand and the turn by `HandMotion`, the change of size by `SizeWatch`, the lock budget and the torch step. Each camera only finds its own focus target and judges whether a lock found what it focused for. A hold gets 2 locks. A lock that fails, or finds nothing, takes the next one, with an auto torch lit first where the camera has one and the scene is dark or the lock failed; once the locks are spent the cycle gives up until the phone moves for 0.6 s and holds steady again, turns, or a new target arrives. A lock or relock counts once the camera takes it, before its trigger is sent, so a trigger that fails at once finds it counted, and a refused one is asked for again; a relock waits for its target, so a page lock's relock waits for a confirmed page. The torch wait ends once the torch is no longer wanted, as when the torch mode changes. Each camera reports every analysed frame's steadiness, so a move between two of the cycle's frames still counts; the frame that restarts the cycle offers no lock, since its target was picked before the move. Without a page, the guide lock waits for the detector's answer to its spot request, which names the request it answers, and takes the centre when the answer has no spot. `GuideSpot` owns that request: a tap, a capture, a page, a move or a restart cancels it, and an answer lost on its way, as a focus candidate dropped for its age, is asked for again, since the detector reports which request it last answered and at which frame.
- With `focusLock` and live corners enabled, the scanner runs one lock per steady hold: the camera's own locking scan, which sweeps until its box is sharp. Continuous autofocus only brings the lens near first. It reports focus on a new scene before it reaches the page, holds a nearer object such as a hand as focused, and can search on without settling, so no lock waits for its report. The lock aims at print. On a confirmed page it scans the page's print spot. Without one it scans the most textured spot of the static guide area around the frame's centre, picked on a steady frame, since a blurred frame keeps more contrast over print than over blank paper: a page too blurred to find usually lies under the guide and is found once that lock sharpens it. A page found under a guide lock whose point lies on it keeps the lock as the page's, without another scan, and the point becomes the page's print spot; a point off the page focused the background, and the page's print takes a lock of its own, counted like any other. The view only says where the lock's point lies; the cycle decides after its turn and move checks, so a page found while the phone turns or moves never keeps a lock that motion releases. A press locks whatever it reports, and a press on a held lock shoots at once. A page whose outline grows or shrinks past the depth of field around the distance the lock focused at, held for 3 frames, locks again on the next steady frame without a release: `FocusController.relock` sends a new trigger while the camera stays in single-shot autofocus, so the sweep starts from the lens where the lock left it and continuous autofocus never takes the lens between, and a press does not accept the old lock meanwhile. A page lock holds until the page is unseen for 1 s or the phone turns about 4 degrees, twice a steady hand's drift, since a tilt moves the page's edges nearer and farther; a move along the page keeps its focus. A guide lock holds 5 s, or until the phone turns or the motion sensors read motion for 0.6 s, longer than a tremor reads as motion. The camera's own report decides a lock: a lock that reports no focus fails, and the cycle takes its next step; no sharpness check of the scanner's own judges it. A locked lens still feeds zero shutter lag, whose buffer accepts locked frames. A trigger the camera takes while still in continuous autofocus freezes the lens without a sweep and is then dropped, though CameraX may report it as a lock. A lock therefore counts only once a frame after its trigger reports it in single-shot autofocus, and `FocusController` sends a lost trigger once more without cancelling, with the camera already in single-shot autofocus, and a scanner lock that loses it again fails as a missed lock. A guide lock found nothing when no page appears within 1 s of its settling. The dark scene is the auto torch's exposure × ISO signal; the text camera has no auto torch. A brief guide dropout does not release the page lock.
- Both cameras lock again on a change of size by the same depth of field. Apparent size is inversely proportional to distance, so a size ratio is the reverse ratio of distance, whatever the page's real size. Near focus, a lens focused at D diopters renders sharp D ± δ diopters, δ = N·c / f² for aperture N, focal length f and circle of confusion c, the sensor's diagonal over 1500. δ is the same at every distance, so the allowed change of size, 1 + δ/D nearer and D / (D − δ) farther, narrows as the lens focuses closer: about 8% at 25 cm and 4% at 12.5 cm on a 6.7 mm f/1.8 lens. D is the frame's `LENS_FOCUS_DISTANCE`, and zoom divides δ, since a crop enlarges the blur. The camera's own `LENS_FOCUS_RANGE` is a fixed range on the SM-G998U, so it is not used. The limit is kept at least 5%, past the jitter of a tracked outline or recognized bounds, and at most 25%, since the reported distance is approximate, and the change must hold for 3 frames in a row. Without a calibrated focus distance, a focal length, an aperture or the sensor size, it is 15%.
- The cycle owns focus for a press that requires it. `ScannerPress` provides one 8 s uptime budget, with one final autofocus attempt at give-up or near the deadline. A guide lock qualifies only when the press needs no page. Capture needs a fresh post-lock frame and, when required, fresh page evidence. Turning off managed focus hands a waiting press to the regular gate. Lens-setting changes defer during exposure.

### Light and ZSL

- `CameraLightController` owns torch and boost commands and confirmations. Auto torch latches for the run and shares that decision with focus recovery. With latency bound it may light while a press waits. With ZSL bound it waits for capture settlement. Manual torch, boost and exposure changes defer during a pending capture. CameraX keeps only the last set of torch and boost on, so the boost is asked off while the torch is lit and on again once it is off.
- CameraX owns ZSL frame selection. Gated ZSL checks buffer eligibility even with optional focus, subject to the fixed-focus exception. Held-lens focus, torch and sustained white-balance instability can bind latency; darkness without boost does only with the experimental `darkLatency` prop, off by default, since a page press waits for its focus lock and for the newest frame the buffer accepts, which keeps frames from before the scan out of the photo. A buffer-starved capture can trigger one latency fallback for the view lifetime. Other light fallbacks are reconsidered on later bindings.

## Text camera

- `HybridTextCameraView` owns a separate preview and RGBA analysis binding. The shared viewport maps the visible OCR region into the analysis crop. ML Kit receives upright pixels. Optical flow carries the newest agreed outline between recognitions. It tracks the outline's own text, or a stretch of a long line around the aim at most 6 line heights long, since flow's fit rejects points along one thin line and a stretch moves as the whole line does. Features are found and the motion fitted on that text alone, so it fills the area the fit must cover, cropped with a quarter of its length to either side and two line heights above and below from the full frame and kept at up to 448 px, so the strokes stay sharp; the crop changes only when the text nears its edge. A frame flow cannot measure, as a new crop or a smeared frame, moves the highlight by the phone's rotation, which the gyroscope measures with the camera's optics and the applied zoom, for at most 0.3 s since the image last placed it: at reading distance sliding the phone moves the image as much as turning it, and the gyroscope sees only the turn. Past that, the highlight stays in place while the motion sensors read the phone still, since the text has not moved, and is otherwise hidden, since its position is no longer known and the aim may have left the text, which clears an ended reading, as does the region's centre leaving text it was on when read, past half a line's height or a twentieth of its length, and recognition returns to its full rate so the next read that boxes the text places it again.
- Main publishes a per-frame snapshot of region, filter, vote revision, zoom admission and focus session. The analyzer owns voting. Main accepts only current binding and revision results. Closing, interruption or failure clears readings and fails pending reads with `camera/unavailable`. A fresh frame restores readiness on reopen.
- `TextZoom` separates the submitted ratio from camera evidence. Each request clears the reading and pauses recognition and the focus cycle. The current request's completion and matching capture metadata establish the frame boundary. When matching metadata is unavailable or does not arrive within the grace period, admission uses the next frame after the latest observed result. Refusal uses the measured ratio when available or the last confirmed ratio.
- With `focusLock`, continuous autofocus follows the region first, and `FocusCycle` decides when the camera's own locking scan runs, the same cycle, sweep and frame-proven lock the document camera uses. Continuous autofocus only brings the lens near while the phone moves: at close range it reports focus it has not reached, and in low light it can search on without settling, so no lock waits for its report, and every steady hold locks. The first settled lock permits reading, since unconfirmed continuous autofocus misreads at close range; after it, reading goes on while a lock is released, since the hand corrects the distance faster than a new scan, and pauses only during a lock's scan, which sweeps the lens. Under the lock, `TextMap` holds the text read around the aim: the region's centre is the selector and the region the tile each recognition reads. Every line a recognition finds is kept with its box, a line read again at the same place gains a read, and one the tile no longer finds goes; lines outside the tile keep theirs. Image flow on the nearest line carries the map between reads. During motion, loss of flow clears the map. The centre selects only within the bounds of a line with two agreeing reads out of its last four. On unagreed text or a gap, the reading clears. OCR runs when coverage changes or the aimed line still needs agreement. A tile that returned text is refreshed after 0.5 s, subject to the recognition rate. Lines expire after 1.5 s without a new OCR position or when they leave the aim's neighbourhood. OCR runs at most twice per second while moving and always respects `recognitionRate`. The lock's first reads run until the aimed line agrees or four reads have run. Main hands the analyzer a `ReadPermission`: the map's lock, which a released lock keeps, and an admission revision that changes with every change of permission, a scan that starts and fails back to the same lock included. Only frames exposed after a lock settles read under it, and a recognition or queued publication from another admission drops, so text read before a scan never shows or resolves `readText()` after it. `ManagedTextReading` owns the analyzer's side: the map, its sensor times, the lock's first-read verdict, the aimed line and its distance baseline, which is the mapped line itself, so moving onto the same text at another size measures afresh. The aim's selection is published once under each admission, empty ones included, so a publication main dropped across a change is repaired on the next frame. Paced and managed OCR each own their reading and tracking: main advances the analysis revision and clears the reading when `focusLock` starts or stops managed OCR, so a recognition in flight from the other mode and its queued highlights drop, and a quick toggle cannot keep the map; focus scans and releases within managed OCR keep it. A recognition republishes the aimed line with its renewed bounds, confidence and votes; between recognitions flow moves only the native highlight. A new lock clears the map. The reading decides only whether focus needs help, never where it is: text often reads while soft, so stopping at the first readable lens position pinned the lens at one distance. A lock found nothing when 4 reads find no agreed text at the aim. A turn of about 4 degrees, twice a steady hand's drift, or 0.6 s of motion, longer than a tremor reads as motion, releases the lock and starts the count over, as new text in the region does. The aimed line growing or shrinking past the depth of field since it was first measured under the lock, as a slow move straight back does without turning or shaking the phone, starts the count over and locks again on the next steady frame without a release, as the document camera's page does. The lock focuses and meters exposure at the region. It needs single-shot autofocus, realtime timestamps, a gyroscope and linear acceleration; otherwise, or when the motion sensors send nothing, continuous autofocus alone follows the region.
- A tap's scan holds the lens for 5 s, then continuous autofocus resumes and the focus cycle starts over. Zoom restarts the focus cycle, since it can switch lenses. Region changes release the lock and reset the cycle and the votes. Filter changes reset votes alone. `RecognitionPace` slows it to once a second after 4 recognitions in a row read the agreed text with the text still, and returns it to `recognitionRate` when a recognition disagrees, the tracked outline moves half its height or a tenth of its width, the phone turns, or a lock's scan ends. Votes also age out on each analyzed frame, not only on new recognitions, so a reading and its highlight end about 1.5 s after the last agreeing recognition even while recognitions are slow. The check runs on frames rather than a timer, so it can run late by however long no frame reaches the analyzer, as during a running recognition. Focus frames from a superseded cycle or region are dropped. [Host integration](../integration.md#text-reading) owns the public props and events.
