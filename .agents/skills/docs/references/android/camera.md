# Android camera

## Document camera

- The view owns CameraX binding, capture admission and presentation. It borrows the host session. Camera use requires an active, attached foreground view. Preview, analysis and still capture share a viewport.
- Capture output and processing use the settings admitted at the press. Live focus and guide settings govern readiness. Rebinding waits for capture settlement.
- [Shared engine](../engine.md) owns guide acceptance and gate contracts. [Android validation](validation.md) owns hardware checks and trace workflows.

### Focus ownership

- `FocusController` owns lens commands, tap/capture ownership and each scan's whole attempt: the frames that prove its lock, tied to the scan's token, one resend of a lost trigger, its deadline and its release. A release asked for during a still waits for the shot to settle, since cancelling would move the lens mid-shot. The page print target is stored in page coordinates so flow can carry it. Metering fits inside the page. If a box cannot fit, focus uses the frame centre.
- Optical flow only carries the guide's outline and paces its inference. Motion for focus and capture comes from the motion sensors, since flow over soft frames, before focus is found or in the dark, reads motion a still hand does not make: the focus cycle's steady hold and the print spot's pick wait for the gyroscope and accelerometer to read quiet over 300 ms, and the capture's framing checks both sensors per frame interval while its smear check reads the gyroscope. The accelerometer is needed because the gyroscope sees rotation, not a move along or toward the page. A running sensor's missing reading is unknown motion, never quiet. Without the accelerometer the focus cycle falls back to the capture's steadiness and framing judges rotation alone; without a gyroscope the capture's motion checks are off.
- The document camera runs the focus cycle `FocusCycle`, with its rules in `FocusRules`: the steady hand and the turn by `HandMotion`, the change of size by `SizeWatch`, the lock budget and the torch step. The text camera shares the motion rules but runs its own cycle, judged by the print's blur ([Text camera](#text-camera)). The document camera finds its own focus target and judges whether a lock found what it focused for. A hold gets 2 locks. A lock that fails, or finds nothing, takes the next one, with an auto torch lit first where the camera has one and the scene is dark or the lock failed; once the locks are spent the cycle gives up until the phone moves for 0.6 s and holds steady again, turns, or a new target arrives. A lock or relock counts once the camera takes it, before its trigger is sent, so a trigger that fails at once finds it counted, and a refused one is asked for again; a relock waits for its target, so a page lock's relock waits for a confirmed page. The torch wait ends once the torch is no longer wanted, as when the torch mode changes. Each camera reports every analysed frame's steadiness, so a move between two of the cycle's frames still counts; the frame that restarts the cycle offers no lock, since its target was picked before the move. Without a page, the guide lock waits for the detector's answer to its spot request, which names the request it answers, and takes the centre when the answer has no spot. `GuideSpot` owns that request: a tap, a capture, a page, a move or a restart cancels it, and an answer lost on its way, as a focus candidate dropped for its age, is asked for again, since the detector reports which request it last answered and at which frame.
- With `focusLock` and live corners enabled, the scanner runs one lock per steady hold: the camera's own locking scan, which sweeps until its box is sharp. Continuous autofocus only brings the lens near first. It reports focus on a new scene before it reaches the page, holds a nearer object such as a hand as focused, and can search on without settling, so no lock waits for its report. The lock aims at print. On a confirmed page it scans the page's print spot. Without one it scans the most textured spot of the static guide area around the frame's centre, picked on a steady frame, since a blurred frame keeps more contrast over print than over blank paper: a page too blurred to find usually lies under the guide and is found once that lock sharpens it. A page found under a guide lock whose point lies on it keeps the lock as the page's, without another scan, and the point becomes the page's print spot; a point off the page focused the background, and the page's print takes a lock of its own, counted like any other. The view only says where the lock's point lies; the cycle decides after its turn and move checks, so a page found while the phone turns or moves never keeps a lock that motion releases. A press locks whatever it reports, and a press on a held lock shoots at once. A page whose outline grows or shrinks past the depth of field around the distance the lock focused at, held for 3 frames, locks again on the next steady frame without a release: `FocusController.relock` sends a new trigger while the camera stays in single-shot autofocus, so the sweep starts from the lens where the lock left it and continuous autofocus never takes the lens between, and a press does not accept the old lock meanwhile. A page lock holds until the page is unseen for 1 s or the phone turns about 4 degrees, twice a steady hand's drift, since a tilt moves the page's edges nearer and farther; a move along the page keeps its focus. A guide lock holds 5 s, or until the phone turns or the motion sensors read motion for 0.6 s, longer than a tremor reads as motion. The camera's own report decides a lock: a lock that reports no focus fails, and the cycle takes its next step; no sharpness check of the scanner's own judges it. A locked lens still feeds zero shutter lag, whose buffer accepts locked frames. A trigger the camera takes while still in continuous autofocus freezes the lens without a sweep and is then dropped, though CameraX may report it as a lock. A lock therefore counts only once a frame after its trigger reports it in single-shot autofocus, and `FocusController` sends a lost trigger once more without cancelling, with the camera already in single-shot autofocus, and a scanner lock that loses it again fails as a missed lock. A guide lock found nothing when no page appears within 1 s of its settling. The dark scene is the auto torch's exposure × ISO signal; the text camera has no auto torch. A brief guide dropout does not release the page lock.
- Both cameras lock again on a change of size by the same depth of field. Apparent size is inversely proportional to distance, so a size ratio is the reverse ratio of distance, whatever the page's real size. Near focus, a lens focused at D diopters renders sharp D ± δ diopters, δ = N·c / f² for aperture N, focal length f and circle of confusion c, the sensor's diagonal over 1500. δ is the same at every distance, so the allowed change of size, 1 + δ/D nearer and D / (D − δ) farther, narrows as the lens focuses closer: about 8% at 25 cm and 4% at 12.5 cm on a 6.7 mm f/1.8 lens. D is the frame's `LENS_FOCUS_DISTANCE`, and zoom divides δ, since a crop enlarges the blur. The camera's own `LENS_FOCUS_RANGE` is a fixed range on the SM-G998U, so it is not used. The limit is kept at least 5%, past the jitter of a tracked outline or recognized bounds, and at most 25%, since the reported distance is approximate, and the change must hold for 3 frames in a row. Without a calibrated focus distance, a focal length, an aperture or the sensor size, it is 15%.
- The cycle owns focus for a press that requires it. `ScannerPress` provides one 8 s uptime budget, with one final autofocus attempt at give-up or near the deadline. A guide lock qualifies only when the press needs no page. Capture needs a fresh post-lock frame and, when required, fresh page evidence. Turning off managed focus hands a waiting press to the regular gate. Lens-setting changes defer during exposure.

### Light and ZSL

- `CameraLightController` owns torch and boost commands and confirmations. Auto torch latches for the run and shares that decision with focus recovery. With latency bound it may light while a press waits. With ZSL bound it waits for capture settlement. Manual torch, boost and exposure changes defer during a pending capture. CameraX keeps only the last set of torch and boost on, so the boost is asked off while the torch is lit and on again once it is off.
- CameraX owns ZSL frame selection. Gated ZSL checks buffer eligibility even with optional focus, subject to the fixed-focus exception. Held-lens focus, torch and sustained white-balance instability can bind latency; darkness without boost does only with the experimental `darkLatency` prop, off by default, since a page press waits for its focus lock and for the newest frame the buffer accepts, which keeps frames from before the scan out of the photo. A buffer-starved capture can trigger one latency fallback for the view lifetime. Other light fallbacks are reconsidered on later bindings.

## Text camera

- `HybridTextCameraView` owns a separate preview and a YUV analysis binding read by its luma plane alone, since OCR, sharpness and the lock's texture aim need no colour; only the region's pixels are read. The shared viewport maps the region into the analysis crop, and ML Kit receives the region's luma turned upright, as a grey bitmap. The overlay draws only the box marking the region; nothing is drawn over the text, so the live path needs no tracking.
- Main publishes a per-frame snapshot of region, revision, zoom admission, focus session and the current focus. A new revision, for a new region, zoom or binding, drops the kept frames and the background read. Closing, interruption or failure fails waiting captures with `camera/unavailable`; a fresh frame restores readiness on reopen.
- `TextZoom` separates the submitted ratio from camera evidence. Each request pauses frames and the focus cycle. The current request's completion and matching capture metadata establish the frame boundary. When matching metadata is unavailable or does not arrive within the grace period, admission uses the next frame after the latest observed result. Refusal uses the measured ratio when available or the last confirmed ratio.
- With `focusLock`, `TextFocusCycle` decides the locks by the print's edge width, since the camera reports focus over blurred print at close range. Continuous autofocus is the first try: while the print is sharp its frames are read and no lock runs, so a sharp hold shows no scan. Print blurred over a steady 0.5 s hold, past continuous autofocus's settling, takes one lock, the same sweep and frame-proven lock as the document camera's, at the region's most textured third, `pickPrintSpot`, else its centre. A hold gets that one lock: a second from the same distance lands the same. It is armed again only when the print turns sharp since the last lock, so the distance changed, when the phone moves for 0.6 s or turns, or for a new region or tap. Blur has no sign, so the lock's history tells which way it went wrong. Print sharp under the lock and then blurred means the phone moved off the lock's distance: the lock is released and armed, and continuous autofocus refocuses. Print blurred from the start with the lens within 0.35 D, the depth of field there, of its nearest focus, `LENS_INFO_MINIMUM_FOCUS_DISTANCE`, is nearer than the lens can focus: the lock stays, the best the lens can do, and moving back brings the print through its focus; the reported distance pins at the limit for anything nearer, which is exactly this case. Print blurred from the start elsewhere means the scan went wrong: the lock is released without another try. A lock's first 0.3 s are not judged, and its frames under motion are not either, since a shake blurs any lens; a move too fast to pass through the lock's sharp distance shows on the motion sensors, which release the lock. A lock that fails leaves the lens to continuous autofocus.
- The motion sensors cannot see the page move under a steady phone, nor the phone move straight nearer or farther, so `ViewWatch` reads the image too. Each frame's region becomes a coarse 24 by 8 grid of mean luma, `regionThumbnail`, compared by correlation, so exposure changing does not count. A frame that differs from the one before is not steady, so a page being moved holds back locks and, moving long enough, releases the lock as the phone moving does. Under a focus, three frames in a row that no longer match its read, the page shifted or the view under half the read's sharpness, drop the read, so a capture does not serve it; whether the lens refocuses is the blur's to tell, since hand drift at close range shifts the view without changing its focus. Frames of a lock's scan, which blurs the view, count as steady.
- The camera reports no blur: a lock reports itself focused at the lens's near limit even over blurred print, and the focus distance stays at the limit however near the print, while ML Kit misreads characters well before it stops finding text. So each frame's region is measured by its edge width, `regionEdgeWidth`: the median distance over which the luma turns from paper to ink across the print's strong edges, 10% to 90% of the way, along rows and columns. Blur spreads an edge whatever the print's size or contrast, so the width means the same in every view where `regionSharpness` only ranks frames of one view. Edges wider than `BlurWatch.CUT`, 3.5 analysis pixels, are blurred: sharp print measures about 2 on the SM-G998U at 1440 by 1080, and the nearest still read right about 3.4. Five such frames in a row turn the box amber and `onBlurChange` true, three sharp ones turn it back; frames of continuous autofocus's sweep, which blurs on its way to focus, and frames with too few edges to measure, as blank paper, change nothing, for the warning and for the focus cycle alike. With diagnostics on, the log gives the edge width about three times a second and with each focused read and capture.
- Each new focus gets a number: a settled lock, the cycle's or a tap's, and continuous autofocus reporting focus. At close range continuous autofocus reports focus it has not reached, so a read counts as readable only when its edges are sharp. Frames queued before a focus began count as out of focus. Frames of a lock's scan, which sweeps the lens, are skipped.
- Every other frame's region is scored by its gradient energy, `regionSharpness`, and `FrameRing` keeps the upright luma crops of the sharpest four of the last second for each focus, ranked only against frames of the same focus, so a new lock's frames are kept however sharp an earlier view was. Once a focus has four frames, `FocusedRead` has the sharpest of them read once in the background, so a smeared frame among them is passed over. That read is the focus's shot.
- `captureTextResult` first looks at what is kept. The current focus's shot serves it at once, without another read, when it is readable, text found with sharp edges, is under a second old, and no kept frame of the last second is a quarter sharper; otherwise the sharpest kept frame of any focus is read then if its edges are sharp, and serves it when it finds text, so a preview that sharpened since the read is what the capture shows. When neither serves, the capture locks the lens once, from where it is, and waits for a readable read of that lock or the cycle's next; a scan already running is the lock it waits for. Only the focus cycle locks, since only the cycle releases a lock again. Past the deadline it reads the sharpest kept frame, sharp or not, and with none fails with `capture/not-ready`. The shot's crop is returned as a greyscale JPEG data URI with its words; nothing is written to storage. Each word keeps ML Kit's corner points, which follow the text's slant where its bounding box only frames it upright. Words are ordered along the text's direction, the median of the lines' angles, so slanted lines still read top to bottom and left to right.
- A tap's scan holds the lens for 5 s, then continuous autofocus resumes and the focus cycle starts over. Zoom restarts the focus cycle, since it can switch lenses.
